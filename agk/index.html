021 01

Structures & Materials

Design philosophies and what each part of the airframe actually carries.

Design philosophies

  • Fail-safe — more than one load-carrying component; the design relies on redundant load paths, so failure of one member does not cause failure of the structure.
  • Safe-life — the component is retired after a declared number of cycles or a time period, whether or not it shows damage.
  • Damage tolerant — damage is permitted provided it is detected before it becomes critical.
  • Redundancy is the duplication, triplication or more of critical systems, so another can take over after a critical failure.

Structural members

  • Semi-monocoque fuselage = skin, frames and stringers. The skin carries shear and pressurisation hoop loads.
  • Stringers help the skin withstand longitudinal compressive loads.
  • Frames give the fuselage its shape and distribute loads; ribs do the same in the wing, maintaining the aerodynamic shape.
  • Sandwich structures are two thin sheets over a light core — strong for their weight, but unsuitable for concentrated loads.
  • Composites are not equally strong in all directions, and they give a higher strength-to-weight ratio than metals.
  • Stress corrosion cracking needs tensile stress and a corrosive environment together.
  • Elastic deformation is temporary and reverses when the load is removed; plastic deformation is permanent.
  • Flutter is avoided by correct mass distribution within the control surface (mass balancing).

Hydraulics, Gear & Brakes

One pressure, one fluid type, and a handful of components that recur in every question.

Hydraulic numbers

Fluid typesynthetic (phosphate ester)
Hydraulic pressureforce / area

  • Fluid must be incompressible, low-flammability, and resist cavitation — synthetic fluid resists cavitation best. It is irritating to eyes and skin.
  • An actuator (jack) converts pressure into linear motion. A pressure relief valve protects against excessive pressure.
  • An accumulator stores energy and supplies an emergency reserve in the event of a hydraulic failure. Pre-charged to 1200 psi in a 3000 psi system, its gauge reads 3000 psi when the system is pressurised.
  • Reservoir level fluctuates with jack displacement and accumulator pressure, even with no leaks.
  • The RAT provides emergency hydraulic power for the flight controls after loss of engine-driven power.
  • An engine-driven pump delivers about six times the volume of an electric pump.
  • Losing the main brake source: an alternate hydraulic system takes over.
  • Autobrake applies the selected level with anti-skid, triggered by main-wheel spin-up and weight-on-wheels logic.
  • A torsion (torque) link prevents rotation of the oleo strut piston. An anti-retract latch blocks the gear lever on the ground.
  • An under-inflated tyre wears at the shoulders; an over-inflated one wears in the centre.
  • Trailing edge flaps on large transports are hydraulically powered with electrical backup.

Electrics

Ohm's law, the AC chain, and what the CSD is for.

Generation

  • A CSD drives the generator at constant speed so that the output frequency stays constant. Combined with the generator it becomes an IDG.
  • If the CSD temperature indicator enters the red arc, the pilot must disconnect it — and that generator is lost for the rest of the flight.
  • Generator output voltage is regulated by field strength.
  • AC generators in parallel must have the same voltage and frequency; reactive load is balanced by the excitation current, real load by the drive.
  • For DC generators to share load, voltages must be almost equal. A DC voltage regulator sits in series with the shunt field coil.
  • The generator breaker closes when generator voltage exceeds battery voltage, and opens when the reverse is true.

Conversion & protection

  • An inverter converts DC into AC. A TRU does the reverse. A relay is an electromagnetically operated switch.
  • A capacitor in an AC circuit makes the voltage lag the current by 90°; increasing frequency at constant voltage increases the current.
  • A thermal current limiter allows a short-term overload before rupturing.
  • An open circuit means loss of continuity, so components cannot function.
  • Pulling the fire shut-off handle opens the exciter control relay and the generator breaker.
  • The APU AC generator supplies three-phase 115/200 V, 400 Hz.

021 04

Powerplant

Follow the gas through the engine once and most of this topic is settled.

Compressor & turbine

  • Compressor stage: velocity decreases, pressure increases. Turbine stator (nozzle guide vanes): velocity increases, static pressure decreases.
  • Axial compressor pressure ratio is set primarily by the number of stages; a centrifugal compressor manages about 12:1.
  • Compressor stall comes from axial velocity too low relative to rotor speed, or from distorted intake airflow. If axial velocity rises relative to blade speed, the stall margin increases.
  • Variable bypass valves prevent fan and LP compressor stall during start and low rotor speeds.
  • The diffuser sits in front of the combustion chamber. An annular chamber gives more favourable thermal load distribution on the HP turbine.
  • EGT is measured at the HP turbine outlet; gas temperature is limited to protect the turbine blades.

Thrust & control

  • Bypass ratio = cold (bypass) mass flow ÷ hot (core) mass flow. 888 ÷ 111 = 8; 200 ÷ 20 = 10.
  • Thrust is indicated by EPR or N1. At constant EPR, thrust decreases as altitude increases.
  • An engine is flat rated if thrust stays constant with rising OAT up to a corner point and then falls.
  • FADEC is powered by its own engine-mounted sources in flight, and includes overspeed and/or EGT protection.
  • On high-bypass engines, reverse thrust reverses only the fan airflow.
  • Maximum Cruise Thrust is not a certified rating.
  • Jet noise is reduced by lowering exhaust velocity — which is why high-bypass engines are quieter.
  • Using bleed air decreases aircraft performance.

Fuel & Oil

Three fuels, one order to remember, and the fuel/oil heat exchanger trick.

FuelFreezing pointNotes
Jet A−40 °CUSA only; high flash point, low volatility
Jet A-1−47 °CStandard commercial fuel worldwide
Jet B−50 °CWide-cut, more volatile, lower flash point

Decreasing freezing point: Jet A, Jet A-1, Jet B. Jet A has a higher freezing point and a higher flash point than Jet B.

  • Cross-feed lets you feed any engine from any tank and adjust fuel distribution.
  • Baffles in the wing tanks restrict fuel movement.
  • A compensated capacitance gauging system compensates for density changes due to temperature.
  • The automatic fuelling shut-off valve stops fuelling at a set level in the tank.
  • Fuel dump systems are required where MTOW is significantly higher than MLW.
  • LP fuel pumps are usually electrically driven centrifugal pumps.
  • Fuel is heated to prevent icing of the LP fuel filter. The fuel-cooled oil cooler does both jobs at once: it cools the oil and heats the fuel.
  • An internal leak in the fuel/oil heat exchanger downstream of the HP pump makes the oil level rise — fuel is at the higher pressure.
  • Fuel flow is measured between the fuel control unit and the burners.
  • The pressurising and dump valve admits fuel to the nozzles only when pressure is sufficient for proper atomisation.
  • A pressure relief valve that does not seat causes low oil pressure.

021 06

Air Conditioning & Pressurisation

The bootstrap cycle explains every air-conditioning question, and the outflow valve explains every pressurisation one.

Pressurisation

  • Cabin pressure is controlled by the outflow valve, which in level flight at constant cabin altitude sits partially open.
  • Cabin altitude = cabin pressure expressed as an altitude. Differential pressure = cabin pressure minus ambient pressure.
  • Maximum differential on a modern transport is about 7–9 psi; for a 41 000 ft certified ceiling, about 9 psi.
  • In automatic mode the controller manages cabin altitude, rate of change of cabin altitude, and the differential pressure limit.
  • If all outflow valves close with packs on, the differential rises to its maximum (the relief valve then protects the structure).
  • A cabin rate of descent means cabin pressure is increasing. If cabin pressure falls, the cabin VSI shows a rate of climb.

Air conditioning

  • Bleed is taken from the low pressure compressor, and from the high pressure compressor when demand requires it.
  • The heat exchangers cool the bleed air before and after the ACM compressor. The ACM causes a pressure and temperature drop.
  • The pack inlet flow valve maintains a constant and sufficient mass flow to ventilate the cabin.
  • Temperature is controlled automatically by comparing outlet temperature with the selected temperature.

021 07

Ice, Fire & Oxygen

Protection systems: what is heated by what, and how detection works.

Ice protection

  • Engine intakes: hot bleed air. Wings on large jets: bleed air. Pneumatic boots: mainly wings on turboprops. Propellers: electrical. Fluid (TKS) systems: wing leading edges.
  • Windscreen heating cycles on and off to hold roughly 18–35 °C.
  • A differential pressure ice detector heats the small holes first; the Smiths detector is activated by an increase in dynamic pressure.
  • AoA vanes are protected by electric heating.
  • Loss of static wick dischargers causes increased background noise and poor radio reception in precipitation.

Fire & oxygen

  • Continuous loop detector: when heated, its resistance decreases and leakage current increases. With no fault protection, an accidental ground triggers the alarm; a crushed element whose core does not touch the casing has no effect.
  • Smoke detectors use a labyrinth optical detector reflecting light onto a photosensitive cell.
  • A tailpipe fire is fuel pooled in the exhaust igniting — visible flame, no engine fire warning.
  • Chemical oxygen generators beat gaseous ones for the cabin: easier maintenance and reduced risk of explosion.
  • The passenger mask is continuous-flow and should not be used if there is smoke in the cabin. PBE protects crew against fumes and noxious gases.
  • A diluter-demand regulator on NORMAL will eventually deliver 100 % oxygen above a certain cabin altitude.

Conventional architecture with manual reversion — the 737 is the classic "cables and hydraulics" answer.

Hydraulics

  • Three systems: A, B and Standby. A and B each have an engine-driven pump plus an electric pump; the Standby system is electrically powered and drives a reduced set of services.
  • An engine-driven pump moves roughly six times the volume of an electric pump.
  • Flight controls have manual reversion — ailerons and elevator can be flown by cable if both systems fail.

Air

  • Bleed from the 5th stage of the compressor, switching to the 9th stage when demand is high. Two packs, one outflow valve.
  • Wing anti-ice and engine cowl anti-ice both use bleed air.

Electrics

  • Two engine-driven IDGs supply 115 V 400 Hz three-phase AC; the APU generator can supply either or both buses. TRUs give 28 V DC.
  • The 737 does not parallel its generators — each bus is fed by one source at a time.

Airframe notes

  • Triple-slotted trailing edge flaps, leading edge slats and Krueger flaps (the Kruegers sit inboard, near the root).
  • Main gear has no doors — the wheels sit exposed in the wells, sealed by the tyres themselves.
  • Engines are pneumatically started from bleed air.

Hydraulics

  • Three systems. Systems 1 and 2 each have an engine-driven pump as the primary source and an electric motor-driven pump as standby.
  • System 3 is fully electric: one electric pump for normal use, a second for high-demand periods.
  • A PTU transfers power between systems — for example after a right engine EDP failure. It transfers power, not fluid.
  • Electric pump power comes from the IDGs, backed up by the APU generator and the RAT.

Flight controls & electrics

  • First-generation E-Jets are partially fly-by-wire: the rudder and the multifunction spoilers are FBW, while ailerons and elevators remain cable-driven to hydraulic actuators. Only the E2 generation is full fly-by-wire — a distinction worth getting right in an interview.
  • Electrical generation is 115 V 400 Hz AC from two engine IDGs, plus an APU generator and a RAT for emergencies.
  • Avionics are Honeywell Primus Epic with five large displays.
