AIP structure

  • GEN — general, national rules. ENR — en-route: holding, approach and departure procedures, airspace. AD — aerodromes.
  • AIRAC — a document released at fixed notification intervals (28 days), so operationally significant changes take effect on common dates.
  • SAR organisation details are published in the AIP; coordination is by the Rescue Coordination Centre.

Certificates & licensing

  • Certificate of Airworthiness is required for any flight operation. Continuing structural integrity is ensured by the State of Design.
  • AOC applies to an operator conducting commercial air operations.
  • Registration marks: at least 30 cm high.
  • Recent experience: 3 take-offs and 3 landings as pilot flying in the last 90 days.
  • Class 1 medical after age 40 in single-pilot commercial operations: validity drops from 12 to 6 months. Only the authority may remove a limitation.
  • An EASA Implementing Rule is hard law; AMC and GM are soft law.

Accidents

  • An accident is fatal or serious injury, substantial damage, or a missing aircraft, occurring between the moment anyone boards and the moment all have disembarked. Landing-gear damage while taxiing with no injury still counts as an accident.

Rules of the air

  • Head-on: both alter heading to the right. Converging: the aircraft with the other on its right gives way. Overtaking: pass on the right.
  • Squawks: 7500 unlawful interference · 7600 communication failure · 7700 distress.
  • Speed adjustments: at or above FL250 in multiples of Mach 0.01; below FL250 in multiples of 10 kt IAS.
  • Changing IFR to VFR is at the commander's request only — never instructed by ATC.
  • Special VFR is not permitted with visibility below 1500 m.
  • In an emergency you may deviate from an ATC clearance, but must notify ATC as soon as possible.

010 03

Aerodromes & Lighting

Light colours are the most reliably asked block in the whole subject, and the hardest to hold from prose. The diagram is worth more than the list.

  • Runway strip includes the runway and the stopway.
  • Stopway is at the end of the TORA; it may be added to ASDA only.
  • Clearway: minimum width 500 ft (150 m), maximum length ½ TORA, included in TODA.
  • Taxiway design ensures the cockpit remains over the centreline while the main gear stays on the pavement.
  • Aerodrome beacon is required when the airfield is used for night operations.
  • PAPI — Precision Approach Path Indicator.

Light signals from the tower

  • Steady green — cleared to land (in flight) / cleared for take-off (ground).
  • Flashing green — return for landing, clearance will follow (in flight) / cleared to taxi (ground).
  • Steady red — give way and continue circling (in flight) / stop (ground).
  • Flashing red — aerodrome unsafe, do not land / taxi clear of the landing area.
  • Flashing white — land at this aerodrome and proceed to apron / return to starting point.

Approach lighting

  • CAT I full system: 900 m of centreline lights with a crossbar at 300 m; the full system has five crossbars.
  • SNOWTAM maximum validity: 8 hours.

Noise abatement departure procedures

  • NADP 1 — noise close to the aerodrome. Climb at V2+10…20, reduce thrust at 800 ft, keep take-off flap, climb to 3000 ft, then accelerate and clean up.

  • Part A general · Part B type specific (contaminated runway data lives here) · Part C aerodromes and routes · Part D training.

The number depends on the number of passenger seats, not on the passengers carried. Water extinguishers suit Class A fires (solid combustibles).

Oxygen

  • Flight deck occupants above 25 000 ft in a pressurised aeroplane: 2 hours of supplemental oxygen.
  • Passenger masks must deploy automatically before cabin altitude exceeds 15 000 ft.
  • At least 10 % more masks than passenger seats.
  • After pressurisation failure: oxygen for the entire period cabin altitude exceeds 15 000 ft, minimum 10 minutes.
  • The oxygen demonstration for a flight planned at FL290 must be completed before take-off.

Crew & equipment

  • Cabin crew: at least one per 50 passenger seats — 100 passengers requires 2.
  • With more than one cabin crew member, the operator nominates one responsible to the commander.
  • ELT must transmit simultaneously on 121.5 and 406 MHz.
  • Aeroplanes over 5700 kg or with 9–200 seats: a crash axe or crowbar on the flight deck.
  • Life rafts must provide survival with the loss of one raft of the largest rated capacity.
  • Ditching, unless stated otherwise: gear up, maximum flap.
  • Seat belts secured for take-off, landing, taxiing, turbulence and emergencies. An infant is 0–2 years.

070 04

Hazards & Emergencies

Depressurisation, contamination, interception, unlawful interference, bird strike, wind shear.

Depressurisation

  • Mist in the cabin with a pressure and temperature drop indicates a rapid depressurisation.
  • The cabin rate-of-change indicator shows a rate of climb when cabin pressure is decreasing.
  • In an explosive decompression at FL410, unsecured objects may be sucked out.

Contamination & de-icing

  • In two-stage de-icing / anti-icing, holdover time starts at the beginning of the second stage.
  • Braking efficiency is reported as poor, medium, good. A friction coefficient of 0.20 means poor.
  • Dynamic hydroplaning depends primarily on the depth of standing water.

Interception & unlawful interference

  • Rocking wings and flashing navigation lights from the interceptor means "you have been intercepted — follow me".
  • Interception frequencies in order: 121.5 MHz, then 243.0 MHz.
  • Unlawful interference with no ATS contact: proceed at a level differing by 300 m (1000 ft) where 2000 ft separation applies, and broadcast warnings on the VHF in use or 121.5.
  • Training for unlawful interference is the responsibility of the operator; security procedures are confirmed to any state from which the operator provides service.

Wind shear & birds

  • Wind shear reported before take-off → delay the take-off. Wind shear encountered during take-off → select TOGA / maximum thrust.
  • Highest bird strike risk is below 500 m. Observing a hazard: inform the local ATS unit as workload allows.
  • Fuel jettison is best done in a straight line at a relatively high flight level.
Instrument failures they slip into this bank

A blocked static source in the descent leaves the altimeter frozen at the reading where the blockage occurred. Iced EPR probes make the EPR read higher than actual, so you set less thrust than you think. Ice blocking a pressure sensor in cruise can make the autothrottle demand less thrust. Flying into a CB, expect erroneous airspeed and altitude indications.

Radiotelephony

Around twenty questions of pure Comms sit inside the Air Law bank. Small subject, quick marks.

Numbers & codes

Readability scale1 to 5

Message priority

  1. Distress (MAYDAY)
  2. Urgency (PAN PAN)
  3. Direction finding
  4. Flight safety — including ATC clearances and instructions
  5. Meteorological
  6. Flight regularity

A MAYDAY call contains: MAYDAY three times, the station addressed, the callsign, the nature of the emergency, and the assistance required.

Phraseology

  • UNABLE — the instruction cannot be complied with. I SAY AGAIN — repetition for emphasis. CHECK — examine a system or procedure.
  • RECYCLE SQUAWK 1240 — reselect the assigned mode and code.
  • Conditional clearance: "BEHIND the landing A320, LINE UP AND WAIT runway 09" — the condition comes first and last.
  • Must be read back: route clearances, runway in use and QNH, SSR codes, level, heading and speed instructions, transition level.
  • QNH 1000 is read digit by digit.
  • Callsign types: registration; operator + registration; operator + flight identification. A callsign like SPEEDBIRD 165 may not be abbreviated.
  • TRANSMITTING BLIND DUE RECEIVER FAILURE is sent on the frequency in use.
  • MSAW — Minimum Safe Altitude Warning. RVR — Runway Visual Range. MLS — Microwave Landing System.
